---
title: Calendar
description: See due dates by month or week, reschedule by dragging, and subscribe from your own calendar
---

The **calendar** shows every task that has a due date, laid out by day. Switch a
project to the calendar view from its header, or open **My Calendar** for a view of
your own tasks across every project.

## Month and week

Toggle between a **month** and a **week** layout. Each task appears as a chip on its
due day, tinted by priority and showing its label colors and assignee avatars, the
same cues as the board. A day with more tasks than fit shows a "+N more" toggle.

## Done and overdue

The calendar reflects a task's state at a glance:

- A **done** task (one in its project's [done column](/docs/products/runa/boards#the-done-column))
  is struck through and dimmed.
- An **overdue** task (past its due date and not done) is marked with a danger
  ring, so slipped work stands out from upcoming work.

## Reschedule by dragging

Drag a task's chip to another day to reschedule it. Rescheduling only changes the
due date; it never moves the task between columns, so its board status is
untouched.

An **unscheduled** rail lists tasks that have no due date. Drag one onto a day to
schedule it, or drag a chip back to the rail to clear its due date.

## My Calendar

**My Calendar** gathers your assigned, due-dated tasks from every project in the
workspace into one place. Opening a task from here jumps to it in its own project.

## Subscribe from your calendar app

Subscribe to a Runa calendar from Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, or any app that
reads iCal feeds, so due dates show alongside the rest of your schedule:

- a **project feed** for one project's tasks
- a **personal feed** for your own tasks across the workspace (the My Calendar feed)

Generate the feed URL from the calendar view. Timed tasks carry their time in the
feed; all-day tasks appear as all-day events. Treat a feed URL as private: anyone
with it can read that calendar.

## Filters

The calendar honors the same **assignee**, **label**, and **priority** filters as
the board, shown as chips you can clear individually.
