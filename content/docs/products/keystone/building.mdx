---
title: Building in Chat
description: Describe a site and refine it turn by turn
---

You build a Keystone site by talking to it. Each message is a turn: Keystone
reads your request together with the current site, revises it, replies with a
short summary of what changed, and refreshes the [live preview](/products/keystone/preview).

## Write a good first prompt

The first turn sets the direction, so give Keystone enough to work with:

- **What the site is for**, for example "a small-batch coffee roaster that sells beans online"
- **The sections or pages you want**, for example "hero, featured beans, the roasting story, and a newsletter signup"
- **The look and feel**, for example "warm and bold, espresso brown and cream with a burnt-orange accent"

If you are not sure where to start, use a finished
[template](/products/keystone/templates) and refine it, or pick a
[starter](/products/keystone/starters) and edit its brief.

## Refine turn by turn

Ask for changes the way you would brief a designer. Keystone keeps everything you
did not ask to change.

- "Make the headline larger and the buttons rounder"
- "Add an About page and link it from the navigation"
- "Switch to a dark theme"
- "Replace the testimonials with a pricing section with three tiers"

After each turn (and when you reopen a site) the builder offers up to four **follow-up suggestions**, such as
**Add an About page**, **Add a contact form**, **Add testimonials**, **Make it
bolder**, **Add an FAQ**, or **Polish for mobile**. Selecting one fills the prompt;
it is sent only when you send it.

While a turn runs, the chat panel shows **Designing your site** (or **Revising
your site**) with a running timer. Larger sites take longer, since every turn
works on the whole site.

The full conversation is saved with the site, so it is still there when you come
back.

## Stop a turn

While a turn runs, the send button becomes **Stop** (on phones, **Stop
generating** also shows over the preview). Stopping ends the turn on Keystone's
side too, so the model stops working on it, and the site stays exactly as it
was. A turn stopped before the model answers spends no credits. Your request
goes back into the prompt, ready to send again or edit.

A site runs one turn at a time. If you send from a second tab while a turn is
still running, Keystone asks you to wait for it to finish or stop it first.

## Try again after a failed turn

If a turn fails for a reason that may pass, such as a dropped connection or a
busy moment, the message under the chat offers **Try again**. The failed turn
is taken off the chat (nothing was saved for it) and your request is back in
the prompt, so **Try again** sends it again, or you can edit it first. Failures
that a retry cannot fix, such as running out of credits, show their own action
instead. See [Troubleshooting](/products/keystone/troubleshooting).

## Version history and undo

Keystone saves a version of the site after every change: each turn, a start
from a template, and each restore. Select the history button in the builder
header (on phones, **Version history** under the chat) to see them, newest
first, each named by the request that made it.

- **Undo last change** goes back one version
- **Restore** brings back any earlier version

Restoring changes the site's pages, theme, header, and footer to that version
and notes the restore in the chat. The restore is saved as a version too, so you
can switch back the same way. The 50 most recent versions are kept per site.
Restoring spends no credits, and it waits while a turn is running. A published
site shows a restored version once you republish.

## Motion, 3D, and scripts

Keystone designs each site with a point of view: type pairing, spacing, color,
and motion drawn from what the site is about. Ask for motion the way you would
brief a designer:

- "Give the hero a slow, glowing shader background in the brand colors"
- "Add a rotating 3D shape to the hero"
- "Make the cards tilt toward the pointer and fade in as I scroll"
- "Turn off the animations"

Motion lives in each page's script. Scripts only enhance a page: every page
still reads and looks complete with scripts off, and every animation honors the
visitor's reduce motion setting. A page can use one outside library,
[three.js](https://threejs.org), for real 3D; anything else is written into the
page itself. Scripts run in the [preview](/products/keystone/preview) and on
[hosted sites](/products/keystone/publishing#scripts-on-hosted-sites), never on
a preview link.

## Title and description

Keystone gives the site a title, a one-sentence description, and a content
language on the first turn. They appear in browser tabs, search results, and
link previews on social media. To change them, ask, for example "Set the site
title to Ember & Bean" or "Give the About page its own description".

To rename the site in Keystone itself (its name under **Your sites** and in the
builder header), select the rename button beside its name in the builder
header, or **Rename** on its card. See
[Manage your sites](/products/keystone/workspaces#manage-your-sites).

## Pick a model

The model picker sits next to the prompt. Each turn uses the model selected when
you send it, so you can switch between turns. See [Models](/products/keystone/models).

## Credits

Every turn you send spends [AI credits](/products/keystone/credits) from the
site's workspace. A turn's cost depends on the model, the size of the site, and
how many pages the change rewrites. Starting from a template, picking a starter, selecting a
follow-up suggestion, switching preview sizes, and downloading your code never
spend credits. Only sending a prompt does.

## Fair use

To keep generation available to everyone, each account can send only so many
prompts in a short window. If you hit the limit, Keystone asks you to slow down;
wait a moment and send again. See [Troubleshooting](/products/keystone/troubleshooting).
